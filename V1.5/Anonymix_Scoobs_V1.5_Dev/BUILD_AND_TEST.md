# Anonymix Scoobs V1.5 — Windows test candidate

This archive contains the complete application source set (9 .cpp files and 14 .h files), the icon, the previously supplied runtime DLLs, and their accompanying notices. It does not contain a Windows executable, a Visual Studio solution, or import libraries. Build it in your existing Visual Studio Community 2026 project before considering a release. This candidate starts from `Anonymix_Scoobs_V1.5_Borderless_Aligned_UI_Windows_Test.zip`; subsequent FFT/hybrid-formant changes are not included.

## Install in your existing Visual Studio project

1. Save a copy of your working Visual Studio project. Extract this archive and replace **all** application .cpp and .h files with the files in src/. Add Prosody.h, PitchJitter.h and Formant.h to the project if your solution does not include newly copied headers automatically. Remove any old application .cpp files that are absent from src/ so no old playback callbacks are compiled.
2. Keep your existing SDL3, SDL3_ttf and PortAudio include directories, import libraries and linker settings. This source set contains no JUCE code or build switch. Choose **Release**, **x64**, and **C++20**. If you use precompiled headers, disable them for these replacement .cpp files unless you have integrated them into the project's precompiled-header setup.
3. Clean and rebuild the solution. Copy runtime/SDL3.dll, runtime/SDL3_ttf.dll, runtime/portaudio.dll and runtime/logo.bmp next to the resulting .exe. Retain runtime/THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt and runtime/LICENSES/ with distributed runtime files. The provided DLLs originate from the prior V1.2 test package (SDL3 3.4.16, SDL3_ttf 3.2.2, and the previously supplied PortAudio build); use notices corresponding to any substituted DLLs.

## Operation

- In **Recording** mode, Start Rec captures a take. Stop recording, choose any combination of **Pitch**, **Formant**, **Timbre**, **Prosody** and **Jitter**, and press the single **Play** button or **Save WAV** button. Turning all five effect buttons off previews the cleaned recording with the current output volume. Effects and sliders can be changed during playback. A saved WAV uses a snapshot of their values at the instant Save is pressed.
- Each DSP effect has one selection button and one slider. Pitch starts at **0 semitones**. Prosody runs from -100% (falling onset accent) through neutral 0% to +100% (rising onset accent). This is a 240 ms, energy-onset-driven pitch and loudness gesture. It does **not** infer words, sentence emphasis, intonation, or linguistic meaning.
- Formant selection now uses the **exact same variable-delay processor** for Stream, recorded Play and Save WAV, with separate state for each path. Playback drains the processor's delayed audio after the source ends; Save WAV skips the neutral 384-sample startup delay and retains the recorded duration. This processor changes the read rate of the audio, including its harmonics and often its perceived pitch. Despite the button name, it does **not** independently move the vocal-tract spectral envelope while holding the fundamental pitch fixed. Sound quality and identity protection have not been established.
- **Jitter** starts OFF with its slider at **0.000**. The old jitter multiplied each audio sample by random volume noise of at most 2%; it did not affect pitch and was unlikely to be audible. This version instead applies a smoothly varying random pitch ratio through the pitch processor, up to approximately +/-2% when the slider reaches 0.020. At lower values the effect may remain subtle, especially in running speech; compare ON and OFF while sustaining a vowel at 0.020. This is an artistic pitch instability, not a measurement or faithful simulation of vocal-fold cycle-to-cycle jitter. Enabling pitch, prosody or jitter in a previously direct audio path also enables the existing pitch delay, which can affect perceived latency.
- In **Stream** mode, the five selections apply to the microphone output. Switch to Recording mode to record, play, or save a take. Hold the pointer over an effect button or its corresponding slider **name** for at least one second to see a short explanation in the lavender information box. The interface targets **60 FPS**; the squares' orbit advances by elapsed time and is intentionally faster. The FFT display uses a 4096-sample window at 48 kHz with logarithmic bands from approximately **20 Hz to 20 kHz**. Its 11.7 Hz bin spacing cannot resolve individual frequencies more finely. This visual window is not an extra audio output buffer.
- The 1430-pixel-wide initial layout places Pitch, Formant, Start Rec and Play across the first button row, then Timbre, Prosody, Jitter and Save WAV across the second row. The three recording/playback/save buttons have the same width and height as the effect selectors. Enabled DSP buttons are dark green with white text; disabled DSP buttons are light grey with dark text. The lavender information box spans exactly the two button rows' collective width (x=660..1410), with the mode and device buttons aligned to its outside edges. Its top and bottom gaps are both 25 pixels. The six slider handles span the same left and right edges as the button group; their captions stay centered above their tracks. The FFT and waveform graphs share the x=320 center line of the upper-left animation. Their backgrounds, buttons and slider tracks have no drawn borders. The two orbiting squares remain at most half-white brightness throughout the animation.
- The FFT calculation matches `Anonymix_Scoobs_V1.5_FFT_Glitch_Reference_Restored_Windows_Test.zip`. The renderer draws the 96 measured frequency bands at 8x window resolution using the existing quarter-pixel contour tessellation, reduces the reusable texture to 4x and 2x, then draws it into the 2x interface target. The final interface pass reduces the complete frame to window size. Each 2:1 reduction uses linear filtering. The approved top-to-bottom colour fill is preserved. The cyan outline reuses the reference core and transparent halo profile with vertical offsets. This keeps the glow visible on flat valleys and peak tips while the outline naturally narrows on steep peak sides. There is no sideways expansion or constant-width stroke; additional raster samples smooth the steep sides. The approved top-to-bottom fill remains unchanged. A monotone cubic outline rounds the tips without exceeding neighboring measured band values or flattening an overshooting segment. Buttons, slider handles, decorations, waveform and text are rendered into a reusable 2x interface target; the font is rasterized at 2x its previous size while retaining the same on-screen dimensions. The opaque interior of buttons, slider diamonds and animated rectangles retains its original colour. Their shapes are drawn without the previous inward alpha fringe or any outline; the 2x interface target smooths their edges. If the interface target cannot be created or selected, the UI draws directly; if the 8x FFT target chain cannot be created, it tries 4x, then 2x, then direct drawing and logs the reason. These visual changes do not add FFT measurements or alter processed audio. The FFT refreshes once per visual frame (up to 60 FPS); measure UI frame timing on your hardware because the larger FFT render target draws more pixels. Each band's FFT-bin magnitudes are averaged; the first 60 visual frames establish per-band noise floors. The filtered bands are normalized against the strongest band in each frame, with a 0.78 exponent to lift lower levels. A weak signal above the calibrated floor can still fill the graph's height; the strongest band's height therefore does not represent absolute loudness. At 48 kHz the 4096-sample FFT resolves bins about 11.7 Hz apart, and neighboring narrow bass display bands can refer to the same physical bin. The two smaller upper-left rectangles orbit the moving center with the original bit-driven glitch offsets; the central rectangle rotates at about 3.8 revolutions per second while the display remains capped at 60 FPS.
- Reopen Audio requests device discovery on a worker after a default-device change. During the transition the display remains active, while the waveform and FFT hold their last input trace. A new recorded preview opens the current default output when Play starts; the live duplex input/output is refreshed with Reopen Audio.

## Windows verification before release

1. Start with the system volume low. Record speech in Recording mode; stop and play with all effects off, then with each of the five effects alone and several combinations. To compare the shared formant algorithm, record a sustained vowel once, turn Pitch, Timbre, Prosody and Jitter OFF, keep Formant ON, and replay the same take at 0.5x, 1.0x and 2.0x. Listen again in Stream with the same selections. The Visual Studio Output window reports the selected effects mask and the formant ratio at playback start. At 1.0x, the recording and export retain the voice after the nominal startup delay. For jitter, compare 0.000 to 0.020 on a sustained vowel in both Play and Stream modes and listen for irregular pitch movement, not loudness noise. During playback, switch selections quickly and confirm there is never a second recorded-output stream or a sudden painful level increase. Move every slider to its neutral value and both extremes. Check that Stop and replay work. Hover each DSP button and slider name for one second; the right description must appear only after that delay and clear when leaving.
2. Save a short take with all effects off, with Formant alone at 0.5x or 2.0x, with jitter alone at 0.020, and with combined effects. Verify that the filename is reported in the Visual Studio Output window, the result is 48 kHz mono PCM WAV with the original take duration, the exported effects can be heard on a sustained vowel, the export does not stall the moving squares, and recorded audio remains available afterward. Try an export while a take is playing.
3. In Stream mode, test every effect alone and combined with pitch and formant. Switch back to Recording, check capture and preview, then reconnect the microphone or speaker and use Reopen Audio. Look for successful device names and underflow/overflow counts in the Output window. Also close the app during playback and during a save.
4. Inspect the FFT with known tones near 30 Hz, 1 kHz, 10 kHz and 19 kHz at a conservative playback level. Microphones and speakers may not reproduce the band edges. Inspect tall FFT peak sides, text edges, slider diamonds and the absence of button, graph and track outlines at the intended window size; resize the window and check that hover and click positions still match what you see. Check the [V1.5 UI] reported FPS and slow-frame counts during normal operation and device reopen, and the [V1.5 PortAudio] callback timing and errors. Measure actual microphone-to-speaker loopback delay externally if latency must be compared to V1.2: callback CPU time and device timing estimates do not measure acoustic latency.

PortAudio asks for a 48 kHz mono stream. Windows shared mode or driver processing can still resample outside the application. The renderer is capped at a 60 FPS target; busy drivers or a slow graphics device can cause missed frames. The speech-onset prosody, formant-named variable-delay processor and export sound quality require your listening test. This candidate has **not** been compiled in Visual Studio or tested with Windows audio hardware in this environment.
